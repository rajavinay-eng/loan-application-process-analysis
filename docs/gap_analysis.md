# Gap analysis

This compares how the loan process works today, based on the 2016 data, with how it should work. Each gap links to a requirement in the BRD.

The "desired state" column is my proposal. The bank would need to agree the targets.

| Area | Current state | Desired state | Gap | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Offer follow-up | Offers sit with no contact. 78% of cancellations happen right at the 30-day mark. | Customers are reminded before the offer expires. | No contact between sending the offer and cancelling it. | Reminders at day 7 and 14 (BR-01) |
| Documents | 69% of validated applications are sent back as incomplete at least once. | Most applications arrive complete the first time. | Customers don't know what's needed upfront. | Checklist at application (BR-02) |
| Document tracking | Customers wait for the bank to tell them what's missing. About 960 applications were cancelled during this back-and-forth. | Customers see what's missing themselves and fix it quickly. | No self-service view of document status. | Upload and status page (BR-03) |
| Online channel | Online applications are approved less (49% vs 65%) and cancelled more (37% vs 26%) than staff-entered ones. | Similar results across both channels. | Online applicants get no personal contact. | Follow-up calls for online applicants (BR-04) |
| Visibility | No ongoing view of where applications get stuck. Findings needed a one-off analysis. | Managers see key figures at any time. | No regular reporting. | Process dashboard (BR-05) |
| Workload | Ten of 142 staff complete about 31% of tasks. | Work is spread in line with each person's role and hours. | No view of workload per person. | Weekly workload report (BR-06) |

## What's not a gap

The bank's own steps are already fast. Checking, accepting and validating an application usually takes less than a day. Speeding these up further wouldn't do much, because most of the delay is time spent waiting on the customer.
